Supply Chain P&L Dashboard

Most supply chain finance teams can tell you total cost — but not which facility, product, or customer is actually driving it. I built an automated pipeline and dashboard that answers that question for a €3.8B EU dairy network, down to EBITAI margin per sales order, refreshed automatically instead of rebuilt by hand in Excel.

The Business Problem

That allocation — transport, storage, and inventory carrying cost split correctly across facility, product, and customer — is usually done manually per report. That makes it slow, inconsistent between analysts, and rarely granular enough to catch problems at the order level.

This project replaces that manual process with one automated pipeline that takes raw ERP-style source tables — facilities, customers, products, transport rate cards, sales orders, internal transfers, and inventory — and produces one fully-allocated profitability record per sales order (about 200k rows), all the way down to EBITAI. The result feeds three dashboard views, each built for a different stakeholder: executives get headline KPIs and trends, network planners get facility-level cost breakdowns, and commercial teams get customer- and channel-level margin rankings.

Key Insights & Findings

With the data unified and allocated consistently, a few patterns stood out that weren't visible when cost and revenue lived in separate reports.

Product category margin is not evenly spread

Ranked by EBITAI margin, Cheese is the strongest category, followed by Powder, then Butter — while UHT sits at a marginal negative margin once transport, storage, and inventory carrying cost are fully allocated. On a Sales Value view alone, UHT can look like a healthy contributor; it's only once cost-to-serve is allocated properly that the erosion shows up. That's a direct candidate for a pricing or network review — the kind of "hidden bleeder" this dashboard was built to surface.

Sales volume peaks every July

Two clear spikes appear in the sales trend — July 2024 and July 2025 — a repeatable seasonal pattern rather than a one-off. For network planning, that's a useful signal: capacity, staffing, and inventory positioning can be planned ahead of a known peak instead of reacting to it.

Channel margin tells a classic cost-to-serve story

Retail has the highest EBITAI margin, followed by Foodservice. Manufacturing and Wholesale sit at the bottom — these are large-order customers with the negotiating leverage to secure lower prices, but the dashboard shows that lower price isn't fully offset by lower cost-to-serve. That's a useful data point for commercial teams weighing volume against margin when negotiating these accounts.

Allocation method

The pipeline is layered in dbt: a staging layer that renames raw tables 1:1 with no logic, an intermediate layer that does the actual cost allocation, and a single mart — fct_sales_pnl — that Looker Studio reads from directly. Each sales order's cost share uses a single allocation ratio (its volume ÷ total facility/product volume), applied consistently across every cost type.

ModelWhat it does
int_facility_inventory_allocRounds weekly inventory up to whole pallets, averages per facility/product, allocates storage, overhead, and inventory carrying cost by inventory share
int_transfer_costsCombines internal transfers with rate-card and origin handling cost
int_outbound_totalsTotal volume leaving each facility, across sales and transfers
int_inbound_inherited_costsPushes transport, handling, storage, and IOC down to the receiving facility from inbound transfers
int_facility_cost_poolCombines direct and inherited costs into one pool per facility/product
GM     = Sales Value − COGS
CBM    = GM − (Transport + Handling + Storage/Overhead)
CBMAI  = CBM − Inventory Carrying Cost
SG&A   = 3.5% × Sales Value
EBITAI = CBMAI − SG&A
