It’s hard to explain in just a few words, so today I am writing this to make sure the next time I am asked, I can provide a better explanation.

What is Consulting?

The consulting job I mention in this post refers to Management Consulting, a type of consultancy where people help management levels with their corporate governance and management. Simply put, when a company runs into situations, problems, or questions that they cannot solve internally, they seek third-party help from someone with specific expertise in that field to guide their decisions.

For example, one of the situations my company helps clients answer is: if they want to build a new cold warehouse facility, where should they locate it to ensure operational costs are minimized (such as transport costs from ports and labor costs) while not being too far from customers in the city center? That’s known as an “Optimal Facility Location” problem.

Different Types of Consulting

I found a framework that divides the consulting landscape into 2 main groups, which pretty much covers almost all parts of the field.

The two main services that consulting companies offer to their clients are: Strategic Consulting (Strategy Consulting) and Management Consulting.

Below is an explanation of the differences between these two types:

For example:

  • A strategic consulting project helps clients answer questions such as: What is the next potential market I should invest in? To enter this market, what steps should I take? Should I buy or build a factory/warehouse in this area?…
  • A management consulting project, in contrast, helps answer operational questions such as: Is our sales performance good? Is my inventory holding cost appropriate? Is there a way to make my delivery operations more efficient?…

Famous companies in the field

People usually use the phrase “MBB, Big 4, and the remaining ones” when talking about consulting companies. So, who exactly are they???

What are the differences between these companies? Following a post on My Consulting Offer, it lies mostly in the project scope that the company focuses on:

  • MBB (McKinsey, BCG, Bain & Company): Focus heavily on strategic projects, and they have the capability to provide consulting services across almost any industry. For example, building an investment strategy for the Southeast Asian market.
  • Big 4 (EY, PwC, KPMG, Deloitte): Focus more on technology implementation projects, or specific operational transformations within a particular industry field. For example, a SAP ERP implementation project for a company.
  • The remaining ones (Boutique/Specialized): Focus more on a local regional market or specialize deeply in a specific niche/industry (FMCG, Tech, E-commerce, etc.). For example, dedicated sales consulting, transport consulting, or inventory optimization consulting…
