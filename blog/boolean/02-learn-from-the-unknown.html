02. Learn from the Unknown

Keep on working and moving forward, even if we are in the fog, the fastest way to get out of this is to keep on walking.

Ngan Ho · January 30, 2024 · 4 min read

I read somewhere about the difference between “School” and “Life”:

  • In school, they give you lessons then you have a test for what you learned.
  • In life, you have the test first, and then later you have your lesson learned.

My university professor told us the story, there are periods in your life, when everything is uncertain, and confusing, like you are in the middle of the fog, not knowing where the street will lead you to. And the success, somehow is the one who can walk slowly, even though he/she is not feeling good about moving forward. Because the fog can’t be there forever, the more you walk, the faster you get out of it.

The stories here linked to one characteristic that I observed in myself when I started my career. Before joining the company, we must finish the personality test, and most Vietnamese staffs are the one who technically very good at what they’re doing but also “follow the road” type of people. Meaning, that when my boss gives me tasks with detailed guidance, it’s very easy for me to execute them. But if my boss gives me a more high-level request, like giving me a problem and I need to be the one to figure out how to analyze the data to solve the problem, how to look at the numbers and find the insights from it, it’s confusing for me at the first place.

It took me a while to learn to adapt to the new culture. Back then, I was asked to estimate the time to do the task and think ahead to what could be the problem I faced along the way to do it. I was too optimistic and confident that in most cases I underestimated the time. As a consequence, when I was just a newbie, I needed to overtime a lot to learn and work for the deadline I set for myself. But the more I work, the more ownership I develop with my task, now I can better plan how long it takes to do and can be more autonomous in deciding the direction to deliver my boss’s expected output. I don’t need to work overtime as before, fortunately.

Work hard & Work smart

Recently I read a note and saw it is very applicable to my experience, so I want to rewrite it to better remember. 2 perspectives sound very contradictory to each other in the first place, but actually, they complement each other.

Work hard, get lucky: If you keep on working hard, your luck will come one day. My professor for Microeconomics once taught us that when the opportunity comes, it will open to everyone, but only the one who is the most well-prepared will get it. The lesson sounds a bit cliche, but I lost a lot of good opportunities when I was young as I was not ready for it yet, and now I learned this lesson. And now, I was working to be ready for the next opportunity.

Work smarter, not harder: But at the same time, you don’t need to work 24/7 and 7 days a week, because it leads to over-exhausting and you will give up. You need to always think about how to make it more smartly, with less effort in less time, as usually, people call it continuous improvement. One reason I like this is not because I was very creative, but because I am sometimes a bit lazy. So if I needed to do one task repetitively, I asked myself, is there any way to do it more smartly?

One story for that is, that there was one occasion when I built a model to simulate the result of multiple scenarios and compare them to choose the best one. The first step of comparison is loading the multiple datasets in multiple scenario data folders to the same name variable in the report. In the first place, I did it manually and every time my boss wanted to adjust the model to make it more sophisticated, it took me more than 1-2 mins to load all the data, and the task was very boring. So I sit back, spending around 1 hour to build a function to automate the data loading part. The result is every time I need to load the data, I just need to press the running button, and the program automatedly loads everything for me. So satisfied!
