12. Sawasdee Kha – A Business Trip to Thailand

Exploring Operation Excellence, learning from a new culture, and understanding the people behind the processes in Thailand.

Ngan Ho · August 25, 2025 · 4 min read

Last month, I traveled to Thailand as part of an Operation Excellence project my company is doing for a dairy manufacturer there.

It was a wonderful experience (sometimes exhausting, but mostly rewarding) because I had the chance to work on a very different type of project compared to my usual ones. Normally, I deal with high-level and strategic projects, but this time I worked more closely with day-to-day operations. I also got to speak with people from a different culture, learn about their challenges, and see their daily work from their perspective.

This hands-on exposure helped me better connect theory and practice, and reminded me that behind every “system” or “process,” there are people dealing with real bottlenecks and frustrations.

What is Operation Excellence?

When you work in a company that focuses on manufacturing or distribution, you usually follow a specific process. For example, for order processing: In daily work, problems can occur along this process, reducing efficiency. For example, if three different stakeholders must confirm an order, it can take too long and affect the company’s service level agreement with the customer.

That’s where the Operation Excellence (OpEx) team comes in. Their role is to identify inefficiencies in the process and suggest improvements by redesigning or simplifying workflows.

In simple terms, OpEx is about making sure people don’t waste unnecessary time or effort on repetitive tasks, and instead focus on what really adds value.

For the employees involved, this kind of project is also an opportunity. They get to step back from their routine and see the “big picture” of how their work fits into the company’s overall process and improve the current one.

What did we do in Thailand?

We organized a four-day workshop at a dairy factory. During this time, we interviewed employees from Supply Planning and Warehouse Operations to map out opportunities for improvement. These opportunities will later be prioritized and developed in the next project phase, called Deployment.

This phase was less about delivering solutions right away and more about listening, mapping, and building trust with the team — which is a crucial step in change management.

While working in Thailand, I noticed two things I truly admired about the people there:

1. Friendly, open, and eager to learn

As a consultant, when you enter a company to “find inefficiencies,” employees often see you as a threat. They may think you are there to judge their work or report negative findings to their boss, which can put them on guard and limit what they share.

In contrast, the staff in Thailand were very friendly, open, and willing to share, even small problems that might not make it into our final report. They were curious to discuss ideas for improving their work, not just waiting for us to suggest solutions.

This openness made the workshop feel more like teamwork than an audit, which is rare and valuable in consulting work.

2. Leadership mindset shapes the team’s attitude

I was especially impressed with the Supply Chain Lead of that company. He guided the discussion between our project team and the operations team with clarity and encouragement. At one point, he said:

“This project is not just about finding inefficiencies that affect business metrics. It’s also about improving our work experience. If you deal with repetitive tasks and believe there’s a better way, please share. It may not become a company-wide initiative, but it could be something we can fix together.”

His clear communication shaped how his team approached the workshop. It showed his leadership skills — encouraging them to think critically, share openly, and challenge the status quo. I really admired his ability to empower people and wished that one day I could develop that same level of influence and leadership.

Other observations about Thailand

Thailand’s infrastructure is very impressive compared to Vietnam:

  • Numerous highways connect different parts of Bangkok and nearby provinces.
  • A diverse public transportation system, including metro and sky trains covering most main roads.
  • Many skyscrapers and luxurious shopping malls.
  • Bangkok also felt more international, with a larger presence of people from different cultures and countries compared to Ho Chi Minh City.
  • At the same time, the wealth gap seemed more visible in Thailand than in Vietnam.
  • And the last but not least, Thai food is so wonderful, just a little bit behind Vietnamese one 😉

That’s all for today! Until next time!
